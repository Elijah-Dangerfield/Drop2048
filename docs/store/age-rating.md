# Age rating and content questionnaires

Answers for Google Play's IARC questionnaire and Apple's age rating questionnaire, with the
reasoning recorded so the next person does not have to re-derive it from the game.

Derived 2026-09-10 for C13.

## Read this first: the kids decision is open and it changes the answers

`OWNER-TODO.md` carries **"Kids theming / age rating"** as an unresolved owner decision, and it has
been carried since before ads were wired. It is not a rating question that the rating questionnaire
settles. It decides the **ad SDK configuration and the consent flow**, and on Apple's side it
decides whether third-party analytics may exist in the app at all.

So this document does not pick an answer for the questions that depend on it. §1 is the content
questionnaire, which is the same on both branches because the game's *content* does not change. §2
is the audience and distribution answers, which are different on each branch and are presented as a
pair. `data-safety.md` §6 is the code-level diff between the branches.

**The single sentence that matters:** the content rating is almost certainly Everyone / 4+ on both
branches. That is not the same as being *for* children, and answering the content questionnaire
does not answer the kids question.

## 1. Content questionnaire, same on both branches

The game is a falling-block puzzle with numbered tiles. There are no characters, no depicted
people, no narrative and no text the player did not type themselves.

| Topic | Answer | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Violence, realistic or cartoon | **No** | Nothing is depicted being harmed. The Bomb block destroys adjacent *blocks* and the row burst clears a row; both are geometric effects on numbered tiles. |
| Blood or gore | **No** | See the glyph note below. |
| Sexual content or nudity | **No** | |
| Profanity or crude humour | **No** | All in-app copy is in `libraries/resources/.../strings.xml` and is instructional. |
| Controlled substances | **No** | |
| Horror or fear themes | **No** | |
| Discrimination or hate | **No** | |
| **Simulated gambling** | **No** | See the glyph note. Nothing in the game wagers anything, there is no chance-based reward, and the rewarded ad is a fixed exchange rather than a draw. |
| **Real-money gambling** | **No** | |
| **Digital purchases** | **Yes** | One non-consumable, `drop2048_pro`, $2.99 (SPEC 12). Android only today; iOS billing is unwired. |
| **Contains ads** | **Yes** | Interstitials between runs and player-initiated rewarded video (SPEC 12). Android only today; iOS serves no ads. |
| Users can interact / user-to-user communication | **No**, with one qualification | There is no chat, no comments, no profiles and no user-generated content of any kind. The qualification is leaderboards, below. |
| Shares user-provided content | **No** | The share sheet emits a plain text summary of a finished run (`libraries/sharing/.../ShareText.kt`). It is generated by the app, handed to the OS share sheet, and the app neither hosts nor transmits it. |
| Shares the user's location | **No** | No location permission on either platform, and `X-Country-Code` is the OS locale rather than a position (`data-safety.md` §2.8). |
| Unrestricted internet access | **No** | The app opens exactly two URLs, both first-party legal pages, and both from a config value the operator controls (`legal.termsUrl`, `legal.privacyUrl`). There is no in-app browser and no arbitrary link surface. |

### The glyph note, worth putting in the free-text field if there is one

Twenty-two achievements are drawn with emoji glyphs (`AchievementCopy.glyph`). Three of them will
look like a wrong answer to somebody reading a screenshot rather than the questionnaire:

- **🩸 (`OnTheBrink`)** is a blood drop, used for surviving ten drops in the danger state. It is
  decoration on a badge, there is no blood in the game, and "Blood" is still correctly answered No.
- **💣 (`DoubleBurst`) and 💥 (`FirstBurst`)** describe the Bomb block and the row burst, which are
  real mechanics that destroy numbered tiles and nothing else.
- **🃏 (`WildFinish`)** is a joker playing card, standing for the Wildcard block. It is the one
  glyph a "simulated gambling" reviewer could snag on. There is no card game and no wager.

These are placeholder art (`OWNER-TODO.md`, "Badge art"). If the real badge set replaces them, the
🩸 and 🃏 are the two worth not reproducing.

## 2. Audience and distribution, which the kids decision splits

### Branch A: general audience, 13+ (what the code implements today)

| Field | Answer |
|---|---|
| Play target age group | 13 and over |
| Play "Designed for Families" | **Do not enrol** |
| Play ads declaration | Yes, contains ads |
| Play "appeals to children" follow-up | No |
| Apple age rating | 4+ (content) |
| Apple Kids Category | **Do not select** |
| AdMob child-directed treatment | `TAG_FOR_CHILD_DIRECTED_TREATMENT_FALSE`, already set (`AdMobAdNetwork.kt:209-216`) |
| `tagForUnderAgeOfConsent` | Deliberately left unset, reasoning in the class KDoc |
| Consent flow | UMP form before the first ad request, already implemented |
| iOS ATT | Not applicable today. Required the moment the ad SDK is linked (`data-safety.md` §7.2) |

**The code is already correct for this branch.** Choosing it costs zero engineering.

**The residual risk is presentation, not code**, and it lands on a decision nobody has made yet. A
13+ declaration next to an icon that reads as preschool is how a Play review flag happens. The icon
does not exist (`OWNER-TODO.md`, "Art"), so this is a live constraint on the art brief rather than
a problem to fix later: **bright is fine, toybox is not.**

Note also the tension inside Branch A: Apple's content rating is **4+** while Play's target
audience is **13+**. Those are different questions and both answers are right. 4+ describes what is
in the app; 13+ describes who it is aimed at and therefore which ad rules apply.

### Branch B: child-directed (Play Families and/or Apple Kids Category)

| Field | Answer |
|---|---|
| Play target age group | Includes under 13 |
| Play "Designed for Families" | Enrol, and meet the Families Ads programme requirements |
| Play ads declaration | Yes, but only from Families-certified SDKs, non-personalised |
| Apple Kids Category | Select, **and then third-party analytics and advertising are banned outright** |
| AdMob child-directed treatment | Flips to `TRUE` |
| `com.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID` | Must be explicitly removed by manifest merge rule |
| Advertising id | Not collected, so that Data safety row disappears |
| COPPA / GDPR-K | Attach. Verifiable parental consent obligations, and UMP alone is no longer sufficient |

**What this branch costs, in engineering, before it can ship:**

1. `:libraries:telemetry` and the Sentry tree compiled out or no-op'd on iOS.
2. The rewarded-ad economy rewritten: SPEC 12's continue hangs off a rewarded video, and
   non-personalised Families inventory changes the revenue it assumes. The Daily retry was the
   second placement and D27 removed it with the mode, so there is one left to rewrite.
3. An answer to whether Game Center survives the Kids Category at all. I could not settle that from
   Apple's published wording. **Not determined.**

**Branch B is not a settings change; it is a different app.** Decide before the live ad units go in.

## 3. What I could not settle

### 3.1 Whether a platform leaderboard counts as "users interact" on Play

Play's IARC questionnaire asks whether users can interact, and the standard reading is chat,
comments, profiles and shared content. A leaderboard that displays other players' platform aliases
is a grey area that Play Games Services apps generally do declare.

**It does not bite today**, because Android has no leaderboard at all: Play Games is out of v1 and
`NoGameServices` reports unavailable from construction (SPEC 15). So the honest Play answer right
now is **No**.

**It becomes a live question the day Play Games is added.** Re-answer the questionnaire in that
release, do not carry this one forward. On Apple the question does not arise the same way, because
Game Center's own rating and moderation are Apple's.

### 3.2 Apple's questionnaire has moved and the wording should be checked at filing time

Apple reworked its age rating bands and questionnaire recently, adding bands and adding questions
about in-app controls, advertising and messaging. Nothing in §1 changes shape under the new
questions, but the exact field names below may not match what App Store Connect shows you.

**Verify against App Store Connect Help, "Set an app age rating", at the time you file.** The
answers are stable; the labels are not.

### 3.3 Whether the Bomb block needs mentioning at all

Answered No to violence above, and I am confident in that: the Bomb destroys numbered tiles on a
grid, there is nothing depicted being harmed, and the genre has shipped this exact mechanic at
Everyone for thirty years. It is recorded here only because it is the one mechanic in the game
whose *name* would make a reviewer look twice, and "we thought about it and here is why not" is
worth more than silence if it is ever queried.
